## 1. The Claims Desk — revise

**Strongest line:** “Reading is a separate form.” A short, occupation-specific refusal converts the player's actual action into a bureaucratic offence. Pell has a usable voice here.

**Weakest line:** “Someone is being fed a calendar.” The metaphor muddies the deduction: who is being fed, and why does a wrong rejection date establish deliberate feeding? Its cleverness outruns the evidence.

**Continuity and knowledge.** “Four million Lumens” is supported by `gloomwing_hunt_00.text`, which already offers that exact bounty. The bible calls Obol the local coin and `src/game/content.js` uses `obols` for transactions, but this candidate did not invent the bounty denomination. Clarify its meaning in the wider ambition; do not call this a new contradiction unique to Pell. “Don't trust the Spire entry date. Nobody who filed it has come back to correct it” is Pell's testimony, not an established omniscient revelation. It needs a reason for him to possess that information and a later means of testing it. Wrong dates are an unratified addition, not something confirmed by the proposed secrets ledger.

The proposed stage-0 Bureau location does conflict with the current route if this is intended as a drop-in replacement: the pack allocates `bureau-hall` stories to stages 20–29; stage 0 success currently unlocks and moves the player to Duskwater. An early Bureau visit can be designed, but its access and onward movement must be specified. This is a route-integration mismatch, not evidence that an early claims desk cannot exist in the fiction.

**Voice and construction.** “CLAIMANTS MUST PRESENT A GLOOMWING” is a clean joke with an immediate obstacle. “That,” says Pell, “was a stamp” is much weaker: he names an object without stating what he caught the player doing. It sounds like a punchline awaiting its setup. The proposed stamp-raising motif is not consistently realised: the introduction stamps without looking, while only the Poise success explicitly lowers it. Do not attach a mechanical gesture to every utterance merely to enforce a motif; decide when interrupting his routine means something. No listed forbidden style construction appears.

**Choice quality.** “Read the rejected-claims ledger upside down” and “Request the claim form” both pay the same progress but reveal different clues; that can work if both clues later change decisions. With no consumers yet, the difference is a promise. The lower-difficulty Poise branch is not provably dominant: its extra-action failure and the different clue prevent that conclusion without actual odds and consumption rules. The forged sleeve mark is materially distinct and connects to scene 3, but a sleeve imprint is not yet an explanation of how one forges a token. “Leave without a form. Free” is valuable agency only if the player can return or find an alternative lead.

**Mechanical gaps and specific fixes.** “(+1 progress; `ledger_dates_wrong`.)” is shorthand rather than the pack's `ambition-progress` effect and explicit flag shape. Define progress target, entry/exit gates, one-time rewards, base action cost and whether “Lose 1 action” is additional to that cost. Declare the item `bureau_stamp_forged` and the proposed flags; provide the later clue/ally consumers and a way back from leaving or failure. Explain what object the sleeve imprint authenticates in scene 3. Retain Pell's short refusals and the sign; replace the unsupported deduction and clarify the stamp failure. This is a useful comic candidate, not a finished exemplar.

## 2. Salt and Wick — revise

**Strongest line:** “One bowl is empty, and the other still steams.” It makes an absence observable without assigning the player a feeling or explaining the mystery.

**Weakest line:** “The ink swims, and you leave with a headache and no answer.” A generic failed-investigation sentence replaces the cottage's particular objects with a stock symptom.

**Continuity and observation.** “The last entry is dated a week from now” is a proposed clue, not proof that time travel or stolen hours are canonical. NB-030 and NB-S40 are unratified and cannot provide that proof. A date can be observed without endorsing its accuracy; preserve that distinction in follow-up. Odile Vane and this cottage are new proposals, with no contradiction established by the inspected content. Duskwater itself is a declared location. The lamp-trimmer occupation is grounded in `src/game/content.js`, but that does not establish Vane's identity or fate.

“The cottage door hangs open on a table laid for two” accidentally places the door on the table. This is a spatial sentence fault, not uncanny architecture supported by the scene. “Along the lintel, four long scratches run well above any door a person could use” also fails to locate its evidence cleanly: a lintel belongs to the very doorway being described. Identify the height and surface coherently before asking the player to deduce the visitor's size. The knife is visible, but the key first appears inside “Take the knife and the key beside the lamp”; establish the key before it becomes a choice.

**Voice and cliché.** Silence is a productive change of register, but “Your hands stop shaking” assumes a prior bodily reaction not established here. “You finish it out of stubbornness” assigns the player a motive, contrary to the style's second-person guidance. Eating an absent person's warm meal could be tender, practical or invasive; the text prematurely picks the psychology. Lamp-oil and iron are familiar gothic tastes; they need a causal relationship to Vane's work or the contamination rather than serving as mood seasoning. No verbatim forbidden construction appears.

**Choice quality.** “Sit at the second place and eat” earns the same progress as examining the dated record, with no stated success cost; investigation additionally gives Dread. Unless eating yields a distinct lead or necessary encounter, it is progress awarded for atmosphere. It risks becoming the obvious safe advancement choice, although actual dominance cannot be established from shorthand. “Take the knife and the key” grants only a key. The absent knife reward is a mismatch between action and result. “The door swings shut behind you, and you hear the latch drop” promises confinement while the supplied effect merely increases Suspicion. The player needs to know whether they are trapped, witnessed, or simply startled.

**Mechanical gaps and specific fixes.** Define why the meal advances this ambition, what each surviving clue changes, and who or what notices the theft. Declare `vane_trimmers_key` and flags, identify the exact later lock, and account for the knife. Provide an escape/recovery transition for the latched-door failure and a return route after leaving. Give inspection failure something specific to this evidence without rewriting it into a success. Resolve costs and repeatability so the warm meal, clue and key cannot be farmed. Preserve the bowls and unfinished ledger; repair spatial staging and imposed psychology before treating this as the quiet reference scene.

## 3. The Price of a Claim — revise substantially

**Strongest line:** “I'd rather you did one of them honestly.” Hesper expresses a preference beyond survival and makes honesty personal rather than a narrator's moral score.

**Weakest line:** “She will remember who carried it.” This is an unsupported future promise and a generic callback placeholder where a particular response should establish the daughter's individuality.

**Continuity and scope.** “It is the only pass the Spire's gate-warden still honours” is narrator-confirmed exclusivity, unlike Pell's warning. The current `gloomwing_hunt_09` opens the route to the Spire through a door yielding “to a record of a debt, rather than iron”; all its success routes unlock and move to `black-spire`, without this token. If this candidate blocks that same entrance, it contradicts the existing access contract unless the integration is deliberately changed. If it concerns a later internal gate, say so. Neither ledger ratifies a gate-warden or exclusive claim-token system. Hesper, her daughter, the infirmary and their relationship remain proposals; no inspected source establishes them as false.

**Voice and cliché.** “She counts her breaths on three fingers and loses track” gives Hesper a bodily activity under pressure. It does not yet explain the proposed motif “always one finger short”; three fingers alone tell us nothing about a missing count. “Hesper laughs until it hurts” is conventional dying-character business; a specific occupational response would distinguish her more. “The girl takes it with both hands. She says nothing” offers the daughter no wish, claim or decision. Her silence is being used to certify the player's conduct. “The daughter will learn it later” is authorial scheduling, not present-tense observed consequence. No listed forbidden phrase appears, but that does not make these strong sentences.

**The dilemma currently has an escape hatch.** “Ask her to sign a copy” grants “both outcomes” on success. Without an irreversible cost, contested acceptance or someone losing something, this becomes a superior resolution for players who can make the Poise check. A harder check is a barrier, not the sacrifice promised by a cruel dilemma. “Neither of them knows” likewise lets the forgery evade the relational conflict, with Dread standing in for a person harmed. Define exactly what “good-enough” means to the daughter and why possessing the token matters to her. Hesper explicitly permits taking it up the Spire, so `hesper_betrayed` mislabels that action unless the player first makes a promise not currently shown. Carrying the token to a missing daughter tests search or circumstances, not evidently Nerve. The Nerve failure also does not say who retains the token.

**Mechanical gaps and specific fixes.** “(+2 progress)” needs a precise stage contract: the current chain uses exact `requires_progress` gates, so an unplanned two-stage increment can skip an encounter. “+1 Scandal in four storylets” needs a defined trigger, one-time firing, journal/feedback and behaviour if the player detours; do not equate four arbitrary plays with four ambition stages. “+1 Scandal avoided later” needs the matching event and mutually exclusive state. Define token ownership, consumption and retry/exit outcomes on every route, including failed delivery, refused copy and detected forgery. Explain how the warden's seal is discovered during a bedside swap and whether discovery occurs now or at a later gate. Declare both real and copied token state and all flags, including `quill_claim_token`, whose shorthand currently leaves item-versus-flag ambiguous.

Keep Hesper's permission and insistence on honesty. Decide whether the scene is about obeying her request, deceiving the daughter or paying for two incompatible obligations; then make the labels and consequences match that decision. Give the daughter a concrete stake, constrain the “both” branch with an actual consequence, and distinguish a later internal gate from the already accessible Spire route. The central encounter is worth retaining, but the supplied mechanics currently undo its advertised cruelty.

## Editorial conclusion

All three merit revision rather than binning. Each has a concrete image or spoken line worth preserving; none should yet be labelled a perfect or mechanically grounded exemplar. The common weakness is promised remembrance without a defined recipient, trigger and scene. This review changes no canon, QA status, approval metadata or runtime content. No tests or publication actions were performed.
