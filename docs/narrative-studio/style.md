# NeathBound Style Guide

## Rhythm
Mix ordinary labour with one impossible detail. Short sentences mark consequence, never replace description.
1. Long: “The clerk holds the seal to the light until the paper yields the impression of a name that is not yours.” Short: “She stops reading.”
2. Long: “Three barges wait under the pier, all registered as having sunk a generation ago.” Short: “Their crews are at work.”
3. Long: “The apprentice counts each lamp twice, once for the street and once for its shadow.” Short: “The tally does not agree.”

## Imagery
Use wet iron, chalk, cracked glass, bureaucratic paper, cold lamplight, salt, old rope and furnace soot. The ordinary physical cost of the city comes first. Surface imagery may appear only as a deliberate, earned contrast. Avoid lush tropical imagery and generic magic mist.

## Dialogue
Each NPC wants something concrete. Distinguish the registrar's careful evasions from a ferryman's practical patience. Prefer purposeful quoted speech and action over explanatory tags such as “she said angrily.” Never flatten all characters into ominous prophecy.

## Humour
Dry institutional absurdity, not puns. Examples:
- “The certificate is valid provided the subject still exists.”
- “The river has submitted a formal objection. In triplicate.”
- “The committee has accepted the absence of evidence as a staffing problem.”
- “Your appeal has been approved for preliminary rejection.”

## Second person
“You” describes observation, decision and cost, not a predetermined emotion, competence or morality. Do not write “You know you are the city's only hope.” Offer two credible choices, each with a reason to take it.

## Forbidden constructions
1. “Little did you know…” 2. “Everything changed forever.” 3. “A shiver runs down your spine.” 4. “Suddenly, without warning…” 5. “You feel an inexplicable sense of…” 6. “The darkness seems to breathe.” 7. “Only time will tell.” 8. “It was all a dream.” 9. “You cannot help but…” 10. “Against all odds, you triumph.”

## The three-pass test
1. **Content:** no typos, repeated filler, point-of-view shifts or borrowed phrasing; at least one striking, setting-specific detail.
2. **Design and continuity:** a clear player objective, at least two genuinely different approaches, meaningful costs, consistent secrecy depth and no contradiction with shipped canon.
3. **Mechanical and accessibility:** valid IDs and stat checks, no dead ends, zero-action exceptions explicit, rewards balanced, mobile/keyboard readable. Human reviewer signs off all three.

The prose editor may only change prose fields. Mechanical revisions return to skeleton stage; a successful spellcheck is not design approval.
