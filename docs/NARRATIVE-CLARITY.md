# Narrative clarity and continuity

## Goal
NeathBound's original premise is a city whose records and lamps remember people differently from the people themselves. The player remembers their identity, but an official registry does not. The writing should preserve uncertainty about **who** changed the records while making clear **what the player knows, why they care, and what can be done next**.

## Rules for future writers
- Provide a concrete **motivation and objective** before an unexplained phenomenon; pay it off with a clue, consequence, or new question.
- Every choice should state what the protagonist believes they are risking, without spoiling surprising results.
- Critical opening clues require a recoverable route. Optional once-only storylets must never strand the main investigation.
- If a result moves the protagonist between districts, prose must describe the physical or narrative journey.
- Story rewards must match inventory and journal: the key is found during escape; the salted map is earned from the cartographer.
- Every important choice should cause a later acknowledgement, not only an immediate stat reward.
- Keep eerie imagery, but anchor scenes in concrete locations, relationships and records.
- Show a persistent current objective plus where to find its storylet; do not assume players will remember a dozen unrelated scenes.
- Preserve previous saves and do not wipe data while repairing navigation.
